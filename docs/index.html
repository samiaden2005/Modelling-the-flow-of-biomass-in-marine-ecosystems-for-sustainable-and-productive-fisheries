Day 41: From a Regime That Collapses to One That Finally Peaks

Three sessions’ worth of follow-up to Day 40’s own mortality-fix discovery. The first closes out the fix’s immediate follow-ups and searches theta/interaction_resource extremes for a clean sustainable-yield curve – finding none of them has one. The second goes looking specifically for a regime that collapses outright, finds it (knife_edge, not theta or interaction_resource, turns out to be the dominant lever), and then discovers that regime doesn’t have a real MSY either – it flickers into extinction rather than settling into one. The third screens 56 candidates from that search and finds the first regime in this whole project with a genuine interior yield peak and a population that’s still actually cycling at it: theta=0.3, interaction_resource=3. Closes with an unrelated but genuinely new discovery: removing cannibalism entirely stops the oscillation, as expected, but under Constant fishing the population turns out to be bistable – two different stable equilibria coexisting at the same effort, confirmed directly via hysteresis rather than inferred from a convergence-time proxy.

Day 27: Day 26’s P-Delay Threshold Was Wrong, and a Fourth Delay Placement That’s Always Possible

Day 26’s ‘genuine threshold D < 0.144’ for the P-only-delay variant was wrong – it checked only that the quadratic’s roots sum to something positive, not that they’re real; the discriminant never overlaps the sum condition, so this placement can’t produce a Hopf bifurcation for any parameters. A fourth delay placement – N(t-tau) in the predator’s intake term – completes the delay-placement space: nondimensionalising collapses it to two ratios (delta = D/m, kappa = aP*/m), giving a Hopf crossing that always exists with a closed-form critical delay, confirmed by simulation and eigenvalue tracking.

Day 18: The Limit Cycle Is a Global Attractor, but Supercritical vs Subcritical Is Still Open

Two experiments today. First: five perturbation magnitudes spanning three orders of magnitude are applied to the mature stock; all converge to the same oscillatory orbit by t = 550, confirming the limit cycle is a stable global attractor. Second: a forward-backward sweep through resource_decrease with diffusion switched off tests for hysteresis. Amplitude grows continuously from zero on the forward sweep, a supercritical signature, but the backward sweep sustains oscillation to a noticeably higher resource_decrease than the forward onset — a gap too large to write off as a sweep-start artefact alone, so a purely supercritical Hopf bifurcation can’t yet be confirmed.

Day 16: The Unstable Fixed Point, the Limit-Cycle Advantage, and What Fishing Does to It

Predictor-corrector integrator shows limit-cycle biomass is ~1.23× the unstable fixed point at λ=2.0 (vs ~1.94× from Euler); bifurcation occurs at λ≈2.08, with the advantage ranging from ~1.28 at λ=1.925 to zero at λ≥2.1. The limit cycle is a cohort wave (2.5× recruits, 0.5× juveniles, 1.5× large adults), persists under all tested fishing effort (0–0.5), and mean biomass drops only 6% while yield scales linearly with no collapse threshold.
